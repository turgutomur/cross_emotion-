Cross-dataset emotion classification suffers from two intertwined
problems: feature distribution shift between heterogeneous corpora and
severe class imbalance with dataset-specific missing classes.
We unify GoEmotions, ISEAR, and WASSA-21 under the Ekman-6 label space
and compare six methods — cross-entropy source-only, mixed training,
domain-adversarial training (DANN), conditional adversarial domain
adaptation (CDAN), and their focal-loss combinations — under two
evaluation protocols (mixed training and leave-one-dataset-out, LODO).
Across three seeds per cell and 1000-resample paired bootstrap testing,
we find a counter-intuitive result: replacing cross-entropy with
focal loss in plain source-only training delivers the largest
cross-dataset gain (\focalwassagain{} macro-F1 points on WASSA-21 LODO,
$p=\focalwassap$), outperforming all domain-adversarial methods and
their focal-loss combinations. Two of three LODO targets are
method-invariant, exhibiting a 47--20 F1-point validation-to-test gap
across all six methods. We argue that the dominant cross-dataset
emotion shift is class-distribution shift, which focal loss targets
directly, rather than feature-distribution shift, which adversarial
methods are designed for. A DeBERTa-v3-large robustness validation
preserves the qualitative finding under elevated seed variance.
